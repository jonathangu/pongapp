## Story contract

- Working names Mara (young mother) and Finn (nine). Mara inherited the Starling and a stopped brass watch from her late father. Finn is curious, sometimes impatient, capable in small ways and genuinely nine—not an adult exposition device.
- Their harbor is no longer safe. Five trapped rescue crews hold bearings for a passage through the closing weather. Protecting Finn and answering other people's calls pull Mara in different directions.
- The dead do not return. Objects, habits and imperfect memories carry affection across generations. The ending belongs to Mara and Finn's new life, not a magical restoration of the old one.
- The existing game remains playable: simple steering, physical tap-to-station navigation, automatic weapons and shared authoritative co-op. Mara and Finn are actual aboard-the-ship characters. Other players can join the crew.
- Authored encounter choices have visible resource effects and remembered consequences. Use existing hull, salvage, meal and map systems rather than inventing an unrelated economy. Choice results, family identities and journal survive save/load, reconnect and region transitions.
- Narrative encounters pause authoritative danger for everyone while the captain decides; spectators see the same scene and result. Guests cannot choose, replay choices, or spoof rewards. No surprise timed reading, no combat behind a blocking dialogue, and a free option always exists.
- A readable logbook holds discovered conversations and keepsakes. Brief contextual shipboard exchanges accompany gameplay. The final conversation reflects earlier choices.
- FTL reference is structural only: ship-and-crew encounters with multiple solutions, as described at https://subsetgames.com/ftl.html . Do not reproduce FTL text, setting or UI.
